Short answer: for the vast majority of users, a **free crypto card** (€0/year) is enough — most of the best cards cost nothing. A **paid card** (Plutus ~€98/year, Revolut Metal ~€175/year) is only worth it if its benefits clearly exceed the subscription: high spending, subscription rebates or premium banking features. Here's how to decide. Data checked 2026.

## Most good cards are free

The point many miss: in our database, **the vast majority of crypto cards are €0/year**, cashback included. Nexo, Bybit, OKX, Bit2Me, Gnosis Pay, MetaMask, Bitpanda… all free. So a paid card only makes sense if it delivers something a free one can't.

## When a paid card is justified

Three cases, and only three:

- **You spend a lot**: a card like Plutus (~€98/year) can reach a high cashback that exceeds the fee — but only at high volume (see our [high-spender guide](/en/blog/best-crypto-card-high-spenders-2026)).
- **You stack subscriptions**: paid cards often rebate Netflix, Spotify, etc. (see [subscriptions](/en/blog/crypto-card-subscriptions-netflix-spotify-2026)). If the total rebated exceeds the fee, it pays off.
- **You want premium banking**: Revolut Metal (~€175/year) isn't a crypto-cashback card but a neobank account (travel, FX, insurance).

## The sum to run

Before paying a subscription, do a simple subtraction:

| Element | Free card | Paid card |
|---|---|---|
| Annual fee | €0 | €98–175/year |
| Cashback / perks | Standard cashback | High cashback + rebates (conditional) |
| Worth it if… | Always | Value of benefits > fee |

**Net return = (cashback + rebates received) − fee − any staking cost.** If the result isn't clearly positive, the free card wins. A quick example: at €1,000 of monthly spend, 1% cashback is about €120 a year — a €98 fee eats most of that unless the paid card delivers a clearly higher rate or extra rebates. And base the sum on your real, not your hoped-for, spending: many people overestimate how much they'll actually route through the card and end up paying a subscription that never breaks even.

## So which should you choose?

- **Normal use** → a **free card**: you pay nothing and already earn cashback. See the [no-fee cards](/en/crypto-card-no-fees).
- **High spender or subscription stacker** → a **paid card** can clear the break-even point, provided you run the numbers on your real usage.
- **You mainly want a bank** → Revolut Metal, knowing it isn't a crypto-cashback card.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are free crypto cards as good as paid ones?**
For most people, yes: the majority of the best cards are €0/year with cashback. A paid one only wins with specific use (high volume, subscriptions, premium banking).

**When is a paid card worth it?**
When the value of its benefits (high cashback, subscription rebates) clearly exceeds the annual fee. Run the numbers on your real spend.

**Is Revolut Metal worth its subscription?**
As a banking product (travel, FX, insurance), maybe. As a crypto-cashback card, no: its rewards are points, not crypto cashback.

**Does a free card have hidden fees?**
Watch FX fees, ATM withdrawal fees and the cashback currency. "Free" refers to the annual fee, not necessarily every usage fee.
