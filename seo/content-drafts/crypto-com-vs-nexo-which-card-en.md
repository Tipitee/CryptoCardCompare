Short answer: **Nexo** is simpler and more predictable (cashback with no staking, one single card), while **Crypto.com** aims higher (up to 5%) but only if you lock up CRO. The right pick comes down to one question: are you willing to tie up capital to earn more cashback?

Here's the comparison, with the numbers, no spin.

## Two opposite philosophies

**Crypto.com** works in **tiers**. The entry card (Midnight Blue) is free, but its cashback is then **0%**. To earn cashback you must **stake CRO** (the native token) for 180 days — and the higher the rate you want, the more you have to lock. Cashback is paid in CRO, a **volatile** token.

**Nexo** offers **one card**, backed by a credit line secured by your crypto. The cashback (up to **2%**) needs **no staking** and is paid in BTC or NEXO. It's far more readable: no tiers, no token to lock.

## The real trade-off

Crypto.com's "5%" isn't a lie, but it's a **conditional ceiling**: it assumes you stake a sizeable amount of CRO, locked, in a token whose price swings. If you don't stake, you're close to **0%**. It's the pattern we see across a whole category of cards in our [2026 study of 95 crypto cards](/etudes/crypto-cards-2026): the advertised rate and the rate actually paid are two different things.

Nexo plays it differently: a **more modest but genuinely accessible** cashback, no capital locked, paid in BTC (less speculative than a niche in-house token). For everyday spending without managing staking, it's the calmer option.

## So which should you choose?
