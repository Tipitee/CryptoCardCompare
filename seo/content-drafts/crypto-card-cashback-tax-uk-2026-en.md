Short answer: **the cashback itself** usually isn't taxed the moment you receive it. What's taxed is what you do **next** with the crypto you received — sell it, swap it, or **spend it**. In the UK, selling, swapping, spending or gifting crypto are all **disposals** for Capital Gains Tax (CGT). This guide explains it in plain English, with a table and an FAQ. It isn't personalised tax advice (see the disclaimer at the end).

## Why crypto cashback matters to HMRC

Most crypto cards pay cashback **in crypto** — often a stablecoin (USDC, USDT), sometimes a native token. While it sits in your wallet, nothing happens. The **taxable event** appears on a **disposal**: selling for pounds, swapping for another coin, or **paying for goods or services**. Yes — tapping a crypto card is a disposal of the crypto used, which can create a capital gain.

## How crypto gains are taxed in the UK (2025/26)

Gains above your allowance are charged to Capital Gains Tax at:

| Your income band | CGT rate on crypto |
|---|---|
| Basic rate | 18 % |
| Higher / additional rate | 24 % |

Every person gets an **annual exempt amount of £3,000** (2025/26): you only pay CGT on gains above it. HMRC works out gains using **Section 104 pooling** and the **30-day (bed-and-breakfasting) rule**, so you can't reset your cost basis by re-buying immediately.

## Disposals you must report

- **Selling** crypto for GBP.
- **Swapping** one crypto for another — yes, even without touching pounds.
- **Spending** crypto on goods or services, **including every crypto-card payment**.
- **Gifting** crypto (except to a spouse or civil partner).

Unlike France or Germany, the UK treats **crypto-to-crypto swaps as disposals** — there's no deferral. Simply **holding** crypto is not a disposal.

## The specific case of cashback

Where cashback gets nuanced: HMRC may treat rewards differently depending on their nature. **Staking and lending rewards** are generally **income** (taxed at receipt on their GBP value, then CGT on later disposal). A **spending cashback** may instead be treated as a discount or rebate on your own spending — but HMRC has no single blanket rule, so the safe approach is to **record the GBP value of every reward at receipt**. That figure becomes your cost basis and decides the gain when you later sell or spend it.

## The stablecoin angle (and spending fiat)

Cashback paid in a **stablecoin** has a practical edge: its value barely moves, so the gain between receiving and spending it is close to zero — and a near-zero gain means near-zero tax. A **volatile native token** can show a real gain (or loss) when used. That's why, in our [comparison tool](/en/compare) and our [2026 crypto card study](/etudes/crypto-cards-2026), we look at **which currency** the cashback is paid in, not just the headline percentage. Many users also top up the card in **GBP** for everyday spending (no disposal) while still earning crypto cashback.

## How to report

- Report through **Self Assessment**, using the **SA108** capital gains pages, by **31 January** after the tax year ends.
- You must report if your gains exceed the £3,000 allowance, or if total proceeds are high even when the gain is small — plus **all crypto income** (staking, etc.).
- Keep records of **every** transaction: date, GBP value, fees. HMRC can ask for years of history.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is crypto card cashback taxable in the UK?**
Not at receipt in most cases. It becomes relevant when you sell or spend the crypto: the gain is subject to CGT (18 % or 24 %) above your £3,000 allowance. Staking-type rewards are usually income at receipt.

**Is spending crypto with a card taxable?**
Yes. Spending crypto is a disposal. If the crypto is worth more than when you acquired it, there's a capital gain to report.

**Are crypto-to-crypto swaps taxed in the UK?**
Yes. Every swap is a disposal — unlike France or Germany, there's no deferral.

**What is the crypto tax-free allowance?**
The CGT annual exempt amount is £3,000 for 2025/26. Gains below it aren't taxed, but you may still need to report.

**Do I report gains on a non-UK exchange like Binance or Bybit?**
Yes. UK residents are taxed on worldwide gains, wherever the exchange is based.
