## What matters for cash withdrawals

Withdrawing cash with a crypto card involves three often-ignored things:

- **The free monthly allowance**: an amount you can withdraw fee-free each month. This is criterion number one.
- **The fee above it**: usually a percentage (~2%) or a flat fee per withdrawal.
- **The taxable event**: withdrawing cash converts crypto to fiat — a potentially taxable disposal (see our tax guides). Spending stablecoins minimises the gain.

## How to minimise withdrawal fees

- **Withdraw once rather than several times**: a single larger withdrawal stays under a cap or limits the number of flat fees.
- **Stay under the monthly allowance**: plan withdrawals so you don't exceed the free amount.
- **Check the ATM operator's own fee too**: some machines add their own charge, independent of your card.
- **Prefer stablecoins** for funding: the conversion carries almost no gain, so it's tax-neutral.

A higher tier (Nexo, Crypto.com) increases the free allowance but may require locking a token — factor that cost in if you withdraw a lot of cash.

## So which should you choose?

- **You withdraw cash regularly** → aim for the biggest **free monthly allowance** (Nexo or Crypto.com tiers).
- **You rarely withdraw** → a free card with no staking is enough; just watch the cap and the fee above it.
- **You want to limit tax** → fund in stablecoins before withdrawing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which crypto card has the cheapest ATM withdrawals?**
The one with the largest free monthly allowance for your profile: Nexo and Crypto.com tiers offer rising caps. Always compare the fee above the cap.

**Are ATM withdrawals free?**
Up to a monthly cap, often yes. Above it, expect ~2% or a flat fee, sometimes on top of the ATM's own charge.

**Is withdrawing cash taxable?**
Withdrawing converts crypto to fiat, which is a disposal. If there's a gain, it's taxable — unless you fund in stablecoins (near-zero gain).

**How do I pay the least in fees?**
Stay under the free allowance, bundle your withdrawals, and check the ATM operator's own fee.
