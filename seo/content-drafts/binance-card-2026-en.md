# Binance Card in 2026: is it available in the UK? (and the best alternatives)

**No — the Binance Card is not available to UK residents in 2026.** Binance is not authorised or registered with the FCA to offer these services in the UK: its former UK arm, Binance Markets Limited, surrendered its FCA permissions in 2023. Separately, Binance also shut down its Visa card across the European Economic Area on 20 December 2023. Either way, if you live in the UK you cannot get a Binance Card today — but several crypto cards do serve UK residents, some with cashback and **no staking required**. Verified August 2026.

## Can UK residents get the Binance Card?

No. Binance does not hold FCA authorisation to offer a card in the UK, and Binance Markets Limited handed back its FCA permissions in 2023. There is no active Binance-branded card programme for UK customers. Your Binance account access may itself be limited depending on the services involved, but the card specifically is not on offer.

If you are searching "Binance card", "Binance card UK" or "Binance card cashback" hoping to apply, the answer is clear: this product is not available to UK residents. Many comparison pages still list it because they haven't been updated — hence the confusion.

## What the Binance Card offered (levels and cashback)

Where it was available, the Binance Card worked on levels tied to how much BNB you held, paying cashback in BNB:

- Up to **8% cashback** at the top level, but only by locking a significant amount of BNB.
- Intermediate tiers around 1–3%, more realistic for most users.
- No annual fee, and spending across a wide range of cryptocurrencies.

The model had a familiar drawback: the headline cashback depended on **staking** (locking volatile capital), which eroded the real return. That is exactly where the 2026 alternatives stand out.

## Tax: how crypto cards are treated in the UK

This is the part almost no comparison explains properly. In the UK, **spending crypto — including via a card — is a disposal for Capital Gains Tax (CGT) purposes.** Each time the card converts your crypto to pounds to pay a merchant, you may realise a gain or loss.

- Gains above the **annual exempt amount** (£3,000 for the 2024/25 tax year) are taxable at the CGT rate that applies to your income band.
- You must keep records of each disposal (date, value in GBP, cost basis) to report gains correctly to HMRC.

A practical tip: topping up the card with **sterling or stablecoins** limits the volatility-driven gain on each payment — though the initial conversion from crypto can itself be a disposal. This is not personal tax advice; check your situation with a qualified adviser. Crypto card providers operating in the UK fall under the **FCA** framework.
